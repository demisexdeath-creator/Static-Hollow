Every creature of Realm 3190 is its own: its model, textures and what glows on it, its animations, moves, AI and sounds. Since 1.74.0 each realm monster has two new attacks, a trick of its own and a better head, and each dies its own way. They all have spawn eggs (in Static Hollow's tab and the Spawn Eggs tab); out of an egg they walk and hunt wherever they're put.

The Peddler and the Hound

The Peddler

The man who sells the doors

A tall man in a grey trench coat and a wide hat, his face always in its shadow, a battered suitcase. He moves into a room you give him, sells the circle, the candles, the tokens and the old computer, minds your things while you're in a realm, and gives you the rewards. He stoops through doorways and sleeps in his bed at night. Nothing hurts him. See the Peddler.

The Hound

Yours, after five realms

The dog in the back of the Archive's photographs: a big gaunt black dog, ribs showing, mangy in patches, pale milky eyes that catch the light, an old collar with a rusted 3190 tag. His bay is a howl that knocks back what he faces and takes the strength out of it.

His ball and whistle (1.75.7): the first time you pet him after that update, he brings them to you. Blow the whistle and he sniffs out the nearest thing worth finding: a Gordon Pike tape you haven't got, a switch nobody's thrown, a breaker on the Annex's closing night, a keepsake, in a realm whatever its puzzle wants, or a cupboard nobody's opened. Since 1.76.0 he also finds tonight's number-station stashes within 64 blocks and digs down to them.

His bed, treats and collars (1.75.8): he sleeps in his bed; a Dog Treat gets a trick (shake, roll over, play dead, chase his tail); four collars change him: Swift (a quarter faster), Guard (tougher hide, harder to knock back), Lucky (digs up better things, more often), Howl (his bay twice as often, and further). Putting one on gives you his old one back.

Where he waits (1.77.3): he doesn't go into Tune-In Town, the Starlight Annex or the Midnight Fair. He lies down outside and sleeps till you come back out. He's 0.6 blocks wide, so he fits through an open door.

The realms' masters

The Stiltman

The Pale Acres

The farm's boy, made "perfect" by people who call themselves the Atelier: drawn out long, his skin swapped for something smooth and rubbery with the seams still showing, a lovely painted face that doesn't move, his Sunday best gone short on him. By day he's at his mirror, humming; come into his room and he'll ask you to leave, and ask again, and then he won't ask. By night he goes out for food, and so do you, in a black bag, if he gets you.

His hands grab you or backhand you across the room; his mouth splits past the painted smile into needle teeth (the bite bleeds); his tongue whips out and hooks in, and when he's got you up to his face it goes into your spine and he drinks (hit him and he drops you). He hears you walk and run, and grows a block taller every night. He rams bolted doors with his head, reaches through, and may draw the bolt himself. Bolt him in his room and he jumps till the floor gives and he drops into the cellar. He stands still in the corn as a scarecrow, takes the stride (one huge step that lands on you), and when he can't get to you his neck stretches down and drags you out. He can't be killed until the five pyres burn. Death: his knees go and he comes down full length.

The Bellhop

The Vacancy

Red uniform, white gloves, a dented desk bell for a head that splits open. He rings before he comes, knocks three times on a door behind you, and is there; he walks you backwards down the hall by the collar. He throws luggage (it slows you), rings for a wake-up call (a ring of sound that knocks you sick and slow and wakes the guests), backs off when he's hurt, and if you run he takes the service entrance: one knock on a door ahead of you, and he comes out of it. He can't be killed; check him out with the five keys. Death: he goes to his knees, his bell still ringing.

The Mire Mother

The Mire

A woman the swamp took and made a nest of. From the waist down she's a giant water bug, a flat brown-black body as big as a cart, four oar-legs and two great catching-legs that strike across the water and snap shut on you; above it she rises drowned and grey in a black dress, milk-white eyes. Her jaw comes off its hinges and the bug's beak comes out when she spews bile. Each sunken bell brings her up to see; after the third she stays. She grasps, slams the water, dives and comes up through you, turns her water to soup that holds you and takes your air, goes for whoever's swimming first, eats her own young to mend, and is frantic when you kill one. Death: she rears up and crashes across the water.

The Lantern Man

The White Woods

A birdcage for a head with a doll's face in it. He holds his lantern out ahead of him: look at its light and you're walked to him. He sends his wisps after you (they follow and freeze), snuffs every light near you, and if you get away he walks into one of his lantern posts and out of the one nearest you. His camp is in the pines to the north, a heap of stolen lanterns in a ring of posts. Once the four are by the fire he comes for the cabin, and his lantern can be broken. Death: his pole goes down first.

The Frozen

The White Woods

One man: a climber in a red parka iced stiff, frostbitten black at the nose and fingers, his nails grown into frozen claws, who did a rite up a mountain long ago and let a frost demon in. He watches you first (up a trunk, hung from the cabin's ceiling, at the window), then asks you the way out: use him for YES, sneak-use for NO. Yes, and he follows you; get him to the fire before he loses patience and the warmth drives the demon out. No, or too long, and he runs, and those nails shred. Angry, he throws icicles, goes up in a whiteout, and badly hurt closes himself in an ice shell only fire gets through. Fire hurts him two and a half times over. Death: he goes over backwards like a statue.

The Archivist

The Source

A body of cables, a cracked monitor for a head with a webcam face on it. It lashes, pours static into you, throws pop-ups (browser windows that open into mouths), screams like a modem, uploads you on a cable and reels you in, crashes old monitors into you, and once, half dead, reboots: down and open for a few seconds, then back up overclocked. Its archive stands round its throne of stacked monitors. Death: it slumps in its cables, sparking.

The Cable Crawler

The Source's house, a hundred hearts

Twenty blocks long: a centipede's body, glossy plates, a cable's copper core at every joint, a leg to each side of every segment, six antennae it talks with, venom claws tipped with a plug's prongs. It's blind: it hears (not wool, not creeping), clicks and listens, and sniffs, and hears you on its live wires even sneaking. It hates light (a torch in your hand keeps it off), but draughts blow lights out, and it spits at your light from the dark. From behind it climbs onto your back and goes for your spine, and walks your body as a husk after your friends. Hit along its body and it sparks. When its hearts are gone it bursts, segment by segment. Its nest is in the back room.

The Wax Man

The Wax House

He did children's parties once, in a candle costume. Tall, in the filthy costume and felt flame hat, his face sealed under smooth wax (two faint yellow eyes, no mouth), blades in his party gloves' fingertips, a camera round his neck. He's in the walls: you hear him crawl and breathe, and see his eyes at the grates. He hears you run from far off, walk from close by, sneak not at all, and talk. He claws, throws you, or lifts you by the neck (spam your inventory key to break free). Held up with friends in the house, he asks where they are. He says CHEESE when you stare (the flash blinds you and he has your photograph) and flicks hot wax. Death: he melts into the floor.

The lesser things

Crowlings

The Pale Acres

Scarecrows with crow skulls for heads. They don't move while you look at them, and caw for the Stiltman when they see you. Round the back, nailed to the crosspiece, is somebody, hollowed out, and the parasite that moves them both. They call the murder, a cloud of crows round your head.

Guests

The Vacancy

Under a bedsheet, what's left of a person. Awake it stalks, crouched and silent, getting behind you; in the dark it sees you and you don't see it, whispering. Catch it and it screams and runs at you, or back into the dark. It claws, retches on you, and throws its sheet over you.

Leechlings

The Mire

The Mire Mother's nymphs: water bugs the size of a cat, hatched wrong, pale shells, a beak like a syringe. They latch on and drink till you knock them off, and wait for each other to come out of the water all at once. Now and then one bites on a static fishing line.

Pop-ups

The Source

Browser windows that open into mouths. Break one and it splits into two smaller ones: "NEW WINDOW!"

The lost

The White Woods

Four different people frozen in the ice, each with their own build, clothes, voice and pace: Daniel Ruiz (19, on a dare), Amy Kessler (24, a nurse out for a run, the fastest), Tom Whitfield (58, a deer hunter) and Rosa Lindqvist (71, a retired teacher who followed the lights). They talk as you walk them in.

The Reflection

The Reflection

The Black Mirror's

It wears a real player's skin with no face (alone, it's you; with others online, three times in four one of your friends). It's a mirror: it does what you do a second late, keeps to the edge of where you're looking, and makes your sounds where you aren't. In the dark it comes out of the glass like a duellist, never the same move twice running: the lunge, the shards, your own blade, the grasp, the swap, the hall of mirrors (four of it, one real), face to face, and your own blow coming back through the glass. In multiplayer only the one who looked into the Black Mirror sees or hears any of it. Death: it cracks, kneels and shatters.

What it leaves (1.75.1): the Looking Glass (use it on something you're looking at, up to 24 blocks, to change places with it; sneak-use to go into the glass for five seconds; in your other hand it sometimes sends part of a blow back; 96 uses, mended with Mirror Shards on an anvil), 3 to 5 Mirror Shards (throw them: they cut and slow), and Only One of You. Four shards round an iron ingot make the Shard Ward (within 48 blocks the doubles and any glimpse crack and go, and the real one is lit up and knocked down); three shards, a glass pane and two sticks make the Mirror Snare (a standing mirror that bursts round whatever comes up to it and turns it on the nearest other creature).

The Host

The Host

The First Night's boss, more health for every player

What came through first, wearing the door: an old doorframe grown into a long grey body, black static where its chest should be, a pale mask for a face. It swipes, slams the boards (a ring of it), reaches with its too-long arm and drags you into the static, screams, and opens the realms' doors on their fragments so their things come through. At two thirds it kneels and seals itself: on every fragment a candle lights, and while one burns it can't be hurt. Put them all out with your hand. At a third the lamps go out and it steps into its own frame and out of the dark behind you.
