How they find you

They don't just know where you are. They see you and they hear you. What they've only heard, they come to look at; what they've seen, they hunt until they've lost it a while; then they search where they lost it, and give up.

  • Sight: a cone ahead of its face, out to its range, with nothing in the way. Shorter the darker it is where you are (under light 5, half; under 2, less again), and much shorter if you're crouched. Crouched in tall plants (grass, ferns, bushes, leaves, crops), it can't make you out past a few blocks. Hidden, not at all.
  • Sound: sprinting, jumping and landing, breaking and placing blocks, doors, fighting and eating are heard round where they were made (the louder, the further), and they come to look. Each piece of Blackout armour quiets you by a fifth; the whole set, and they can't hear you at all (Radio Silence).
  • Your heartbeat: the nearer one is to finding you, the faster and louder it goes, and his static creeps in at the edges of your eyes.

Hiding

  • A Steel Locker (iron and bars): use it and you're in, pulling the door to. You see out through its vents, and nothing sees in. Crouch to get out (or use it again).
  • Under a bed: crouch and use any bed and you slide under it. Crouch to come out.

One that saw you go in, or hears you in there (any noise you make), tears it open and drags you out. One that's only looking for you comes and stands at it a while, sniffing, listening, and goes. Sometimes it pulls it open anyway.

They hunt together

Since 1.64.1:

  • The call: one sees you, its head snaps round and it shrieks, and every one of his for thirty blocks that isn't already after someone comes to look.
  • The surround: those after you don't all come down the same line. Each takes its own side of you, spread round you, and closes in from there (the last few blocks straight at you).
  • The cut-off: they don't run to where you are, they run to where you're going. The faster you're going, the further ahead of you they aim.
  • The search: lost, they check the lockers and beds round where they lost you, stood at each, listening.
  • Far off, now and then, one just stands and stares at you.

Hunting, they beat in wooden doors (not iron). Since 1.67.0 they climb walls after you if you're above them (not the servants), and stuck for a couple of seconds they claw through what's in the way if it's wood, glass, earth, leaves or wool: cracks spread across it, a few seconds' work. Stone and iron hold them, they never touch anything with a chest in it, and with mobGriefing off they never break anything. Or they try some other way round.

At dawn, those out under the sky go off the air (static, and gone), unless they're hunting you. The ones underground stay, and so do his servants and the Lookalikes.

When they come

The first of them come for you a few seconds after the Broadcast, near home. Every night after (and any time you're down in the caves) the broadcast goes out round you, a tone far off, and they come: more each night you've lived through, up to ten, out of the dark and out of your sight. By day, Lookalikes, near villages and herds. Now and then one of his servants comes at the head of a night's broadcast.

The Watcher stays at your house, and strikes down anything of his that comes near it. A Lookalike can fool him.

His sites come through into your world after it, each kept by one of his servants, each with a Broadcast Core: break it and the site goes off the air (Pulled the Plug), and for a minute his signal's torn open where it stood: a way into his kingdom. A keeper you've killed doesn't come back. More on Chapter Ten.

His creations

The Longplay

A person recorded over too many times: drawn out tall and thin like a chewed-up tape, eleven feet tall, tracking lines through it, its face smeared to a blur with one dark mouth in it. Out at the edge of the light, between the trees.

Watched, it pauses: stopped dead, mid-step, the picture of it juddering like a paused tape. Look away and it plays again, fast-forward: nearer every time you look back. Stare at it too long and the tape slips and a frame's skipped: it's nearer, though nobody saw it move. It comes at you from behind, and in reach and unwatched it takes you with both long hands.

  • The Lash (1.67.0): a loop of its tape whipped out at you from up to twelve blocks, round your legs, and you're reeled in, slowed.
  • Rewind (1.67.0): hurt past half, it plays itself back to where it was a couple of seconds ago, a little mended.

Its death: its tape runs out. It's hauled up, drawn out tall, and folds up onto its face, ribbon spilling.

The Hanger

A person folded the wrong way: too many joints, its arms and legs bent back over it, its head hung upside down under its body. Fast, and fragile. It goes up walls and over ceilings. In the caves (and in the trees, at night) it hangs from the roof over a way through, still, and drops on whoever passes under it. It jinks as it comes, bites, and runs off up the walls into the dark to come again. Lost, it goes up onto the roof over where it lost you, and waits. His burrows have Hangers on their roofs.

  • The Hoist (1.67.0): hung from a roof right over you, it reaches down, lifts you up to it, bites, and lets go (and maybe comes down after you).
  • The Pounce (1.67.0): from six to eleven blocks off, wound down low, a leap that pins you.

Its death: it flips onto its back and curls up like a dead spider, twitching.

The Lookalike

A copy. A villager, a cow, a sheep, a pig, a chicken: the Watcher. Only a frame out: its picture a beat behind itself, its sounds played backwards, and now and then, for a frame, what it really is. Lookalikes walk by day too.

It wanders up to you, being what it copies, closer every time you look away. It shies away from you into the dark like an animal (so you'll follow), and lets itself in through your doors. Near enough, or hit, it opens, down its middle, the two halves of it swinging apart, and what's in there leaps at you. On his sets they stand about being people.

  • The Embrace (1.67.0): its leap lands on you and it closes round you, holding on, biting. Hit it and it lets go.
  • The Lure (1.67.0): disguised, it throws its voice off to one side of you.

Its death: flung open, on its knees, over backwards.

ERR

The broken script. Its name in game is a row of boxes, "▯▯▯". A figure in slices that don't line up, patches of it the colour of a picture that's missing. It skips: it isn't anywhere in between, it's just further on, a frame at a time. It skips to where you're going, and out of your sight it comes up behind you, whispering. It can only be hurt in the frames it's all there. Rare, and worse every night it's let out.

Its death: it comes to pieces, then the picture collapses, a line, a dot: "[Server thread/INFO]: ▯▯▯ has left the game".

His servants

The three at his war table. He sends them after his creations, one at the head of a night's broadcast now and then. Big and slow to die, each with its own way of finding you and finishing you, and each a boss: its bar's up while it's near. Each keeps one of his sites. Killing one earns Off His Air.

The Announcer

Tall, in a long coat, an old radio's speaker grille for a face, its dial glowing, a microphone in its hand on a cable that goes back into it. It keeps his Transmitter. Its voice knocks you down: a blast of it out in front of it, everything in the way thrown back, deafened, sick. It keeps you at the edge of its voice, circling, and calls everything of his for 64 blocks in: "STAY TUNED." It says other things too: "WE INTERRUPT THIS PROGRAMME." "PLEASE DO NOT ADJUST YOUR SET." "YOU ARE ON AIR." "THIS IS NOT A TEST."

  • The Cable (1.67.0): its microphone cable thrown round you, and you're reeled in in front of it, into its blast.
  • Feedback (1.67.0): too close, a howl all round it throws everyone off, sick.

Its death: it clutches its head, staggers, kneels and falls. "WE ARE EXPERIENCING TECHNICAL DIFFICULTIES."

The Cameraman

An old studio camera for a head with a red tally light on it, and three legs like its tripod's. It keeps his Burrow. While it films you, everything knows where you are: its light comes on, and every one of his creations for a long way round comes for you. Its flash blinds you. It keeps you in frame, backing off if you come at it, round the corner after you.

  • The Zoom (1.67.0): a sudden rush in from its frame. "CUT."
  • The Slam (1.67.0): reared up, its front leg driven down, and the ground jumps under you.

Its death: its legs buckle one by one, its lens goes, and it tips over. "CUT."

The Stagehand

Big, a sack over its head with a slit in it and a light behind the slit, coils of rope over it, sandbags hung off its belt, one hand a fist and the other a rigging hook. It keeps his Set. It throws the hook, drags you in, and brings its fist down: the ground jumps. It charges: sidestep it and it goes into the wall behind you, dazed and wide open (it takes half as much again).

  • Sandbags (1.67.0): three heaved up high over you, the ground marked where each will land. Get out from under.
  • Cue the Dark (1.67.0): it yanks a rope and your eyes go dark, and in the dark it charges.

Its death: it drops its hook, kneels, and goes over like a tree.

Since 1.64.1 all seven have their own horrible sounds (a children's presenter's voice on a chewed tape, laughter played backwards, many mouths screaming at once, joints going the wrong way, a modem's scream, a PA's off-key chime, whispering behind you), and every death ends the same: static boiling off it, the thump of it hitting the floor, the picture of it shaking as it goes.

The Faithful

His congregation, in his castle (since 1.64.2). Tall, in hooded robes of a red so dark it's nearly black, a little red light hung at the breast, and under the hood nothing: a black hole where a face goes, a couple of specks of red far back in it. They keep his rites: kneeling in circles of red and black candles round a red lamp set in the floor, rocking, chanting (its words nobody's language); praising on their knees with their arms up to him, swaying; bowed right down flat, arms out along the floor; walking the causeway and the courtyard in slow procession, hands clasped, hoods bowed. Two circles in the courtyard, four in the war hall's aisles behind the pillars, eight kneeling at his war table (four a side, behind his servants), three walking the causeway and three round the courtyard.

They don't stop for you. But their hoods turn after you, wherever you go, further round than a head should. Come right up to one and the whole circle stops: on their feet, silent, every hood on you, the nearest whispering at you, till you go. Hurt one and every one of them for 24 blocks turns, the chant breaks into a scream, and they run at you, arms out, clawing, their touch putting your eyes out. Get far enough away and they go back to their places and kneel again.

Their death: the robe falls empty. It reels, its hood drops in on itself, and there's nothing in it: it all goes down in a heap of cloth, sleeves flat out, smoke and static coming off it. They're set down once in every world his castle's in, and those killed stay dead. Sometimes they drop the Faithful's Dagger he made them ("What his Faithful give themselves to him with"), a quick blade.

In the Broadcast they're down on their faces as he goes by. At the Coronation they give themselves to him with their daggers, and his armour forms out of them; at the Forge they close its cracks and chant their glyphs into it; at the Second Rite they sing round him, "head to crown him. wings to bear him." / "heart to keep him. ours, for ever.", and in the end they seize him and take him into a seal. See the First Signal.

SENTINEL's crew

SENTINEL Crew, 20 health, only in THE LAST BROADCAST's third part. When he tears SENTINEL's right side off, a module of it comes down in his sea, and its astronauts climb out of its hatch onto its hull, in white suits and gold visors, their packs on their backs, their radio breaking up:

SENTINEL brought Blackwater's parasites up with it, and they're out, onto the crew one after another. The last of them backs off to the end of the hull, and the parasites stop, and the Chorus's last voice speaks through them: "...one voice left." "it will do." They pour into him, and the hive comes up through him: "we hear you, signal." Then the Harbinger swallows him whole.

The End's guardian

The End's Guardian, Taken (since 1.71.0): the Ender Dragon with the parasite all through it, the hive's now, its back split open and the hive's growths on it. When he tears his arena up into the End, it comes up out of the dark and circles it.

It has 300 health, but it can't be brought down (the hive keeps it going); only he can do that. Now and then it comes round at one of you and strafes you, its fireballs bursting into its breath on the floor, or dives at you, low and fast, and through. Hurt, it screams. At the Second Rite he rides it down and takes its head, its wings and its heart for himself. It's a creature of its own, apart from the Infested Ender Dragon.

How to survive them

  • At night, keep to the dark and crouch. Crouched in tall grass, they can't make you out past a few blocks.
  • Don't sprint, don't jump, don't break or place things near them. Each piece of Blackout armour makes you quieter.
  • When your heartbeat races, one's close to finding you. Get into a Steel Locker or under a bed before it sees you go in, and keep still.
  • Build in stone or iron, with iron doors: they claw through wood, glass, earth, leaves and wool.
  • Kill a Cameraman before anything else: while it films you, everything knows where you are.
  • Sidestep the Stagehand's charge into a wall, then hit it.
  • Last till dawn: most of the ones out under the sky go off the air (not one that's after you).
  • Read the programme as the night comes in, and don't open the door on THE IMPOSTORS.
