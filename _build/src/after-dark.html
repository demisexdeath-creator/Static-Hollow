Since 1.75.9 the Hollow has more going on after dark: things you can carry (a camcorder, a shortwave radio), places that come and go (Crane's Midnight Fair), and things that come looking for you (payphones that ring, a storm that isn't weather, a bus at 3:19 in the morning, an ice cream truck whose tune goes wrong). Most of it ties together: the radio's stations point to buried things, the payphones' callers are people you've met, and the last call starts the Final Broadcast.

They wait for the story

Since 1.77.3 none of these come looking for you until the story's done (the Last Broadcast has signed off): the fair's gate, the payphones ringing, the static storms, the Herald, the night bus, the ice cream truck and the seasons. Things you've already found or made stay. /statichollow eggs early opens them in a world now; /statichollow eggs story goes back to waiting.

The camcorder

Use the Camcorder to start recording, and again to stop. The viewfinder comes up: REC, the timecode, the battery, the grain, the scanlines, night vision's green. A Camcorder Battery is twenty minutes of recording; sneak-use the camcorder with a spare on you (or craft it with one) to put a fresh one in.

  • On tape: hold one of Realm 3190's things in the frame for three seconds and it's on tape. Its page in the Bestiary says so and you get VHS Footage of it (On Tape; every one of them, The Whole Collection).
  • Watch it back: put a tape in the VCR television, or use the TV empty-handed to watch everything you've filmed, turning in the static: PLAY, the counter, the tracking going now and then. With more than one tape, the arrows go through them.
  • Only on camera: writing on the walls that nobody else can see, a few scrawled words that are gone in a few minutes; and the Dropout.

The Dropout

A gap on the tape in somebody's shape, smeared across the frame like a double exposure, and only there. Nobody sees it without a camcorder recording; you only hear it close, a hiss like a dead channel. It comes for whoever carries a camcorder, at night and in the realms: walking, then quicker, never stopping, unless it's in that player's frame, when it slows to a crawl and stutters. If it reaches you it goes right through you: the dark, the cold, and it's gone. Nothing hurts it; it fades on its own in a few minutes.

The shortwave radio

Use the Shortwave Radio and its dial comes up. Turn it (scroll, drag, the arrow keys; shift for big steps) through the band, 3000 to 9999 kHz, through the hiss (Night Frequencies). Close to a station the static thins and the meter's needle lifts; settle on it and you hear what's there. After dark there's somebody at a handful of places on the dial, different in every world:

  • Three number stations: a flat voice reading two numbers over and over, the X and the Z of somewhere out there with something buried at it: a chest, two blocks down. A new one each night, each station. The Hound can sniff them out.
  • WHOL, the town's old station: its broadcasts, from the night the first television signal went out to the night the fair didn't arrive in Millbrook, and its signing-off.
  • The Night Owl: help for anyone lost in one of the realms' houses, a room at a time.
  • The listener, somewhere different each night: breathing. Stay on it and it hears you; stay long enough and it reads you where you're standing, and it comes (It Was Listening Too).
  • In a static storm, one more station: WHOL's emergency bulletin, with the listener in between.

Static fishing

Cast into the water in one of the realms, in Pearl's pool in the Starlight Annex, at the Midnight Fair, or anywhere at night with a Static Lure in your other hand, and what bites isn't what lives in ordinary water:

  • Strange fish (Gloomfin, Eyeball Carp, Tapefish and more), each with something odd about eating it: the Gloomfin lets you see in the dark a while, the Eyeball Carp makes you glow, the Tapefish is lucky. Every kind earns Strange Waters.
  • Junk off the bottom, like a Soggy VHS Tape.
  • Now and then an odd catch: a missing person's keepsake you haven't found, or one of the realms' tapes.
  • Now and then something that bites: a leechling, and it's angry.

Each kind goes in the Journal's fishing page once you've had it.

The Curator

Some nights (1.77.0), when you're in your collector's hall with three or more of your own cases near, an old man in a museum guard's coat and white gloves is there too (After Hours). He walks from case to case with his lamp, telling you about each set; for every set that's whole, a Curator's Ribbon, "with the museum's compliments" (With the Museum's Compliments). Then he switches on the VCR televisions near the cases, so your footage plays on them (use one), and goes. He's harmless. He never says where he came from.

The payphones

Since 1.76.0 there are phone booths out in the Hollow that weren't there before. One turns up now and then a little way off from someone out after dark, always somewhere open and level and never in or near anything anyone's built. You can put one up yourself too. At night they ring. Pick up (Is Anybody There?): somebody with something to tell you, and no other way left to say it. One call a night, in order:

  1. Gordon Pike, the Annex's stage manager
  2. Old Nell, about the storms
  3. The night desk: the numbers, and tonight's first stash
  4. Jimmy Mercer, still in the corn
  5. Chef Gustave, about his kitchen
  6. An unknown caller: the listener
  7. Augustin Crane
  8. WHOL itself, once Crane's free: "At midnight, tune your set to 1962."

Each call you take you keep on an Answering Machine Tape (play it to hear the call again); all eight are a set for the collector's hall (Collect Calls). Once they're all taken, the night desk still rings some nights with a stash.

The call-in line

Since 1.77.0, pick up a phone that isn't ringing and dial (Caller, You're On the Air):

  • Request a song: the DJ introduces it, and a record plays from the phone.
  • Ask the Night Owl: a hint about what's next for you.
  • Dial the number from the radio (if you've heard the numbers tonight): whoever buried it picks up, and there's another stash.
  • Dial 3190: the Eavesdropper, if it isn't beaten yet. After that, Gordon picks up to thank you.

Static storms

Some nights (about one in five, never a fair night), or when a thunderstorm comes up after dark, the sky goes the grey of a dead channel and crackles, and a fine snow of static falls over everything, now and then a tear rolling across (Dead Channel Sky). For five minutes:

  • the shortwave picks up a station that's only there in a storm, and anyone with a radio is told where it is;
  • the camcorder shows more: the writing, the Dropouts;
  • Storm Wisps come down out of it: knots of crackling static the size of a head, trailing threads, that drift, close on somebody out in it and zap them (a shock and a moment's slowness), then pull away and come round again. A few hits put one out; they go when the storm does.
  • the lightning leaves its glass where it strikes: a fulgurite of what it hit (Sand, Rock, Earth, Frost, and now and then a Singing one). Five for the collector's hall.

The Hollow Herald forecasts tonight's storm.

The Final Broadcast

Once Augustin Crane's free and you've taken WHOL's call, it's on. At midnight, out in the open, tune the shortwave down to 1.962 MHz. WHOL comes on the air for the last time: what's been living on the frequency for sixty years and how it feeds, and Crane, who promised it a show. You're told to turn your set all the way up and put it down (use the radio on the ground: the broadcast radio). Then it comes for the loudest radio in the Hollow, and the storm with it.

The Eavesdropper

The Final Broadcast's boss

What's been living on WHOL's frequency: very tall and thin and stooped, a dead channel's snow for a body, no eyes, its long hands cupped behind two enormous ears, and a cord running from the back of its neck down into the ground. It comes slowly for the radio, to switch it off. The radio's noise wears it away; hit it and it staggers back, screaming static; let it stand at the radio long enough and the radio's off, and that's it till another night. Wear it away to nothing and WHOL signs off for good (Good Night, Static Hollow): WHOL's Microphone for your collector's hall, and the listener's station is only hiss from then on. On the Phantom Express's wrong turn it's there for a moment, at the end of the dark.

The Hollow Herald

Every morning since 1.77.0, a paper for each of you, about yesterday (Extra! Extra!), left at your bed or put in your pack. It opens like the Journal:

  • the front page: what you did, from everything that happened to you (the fair, Crane, a storm, the glass, what you filmed, a dish, a call, the ghost train, the bus, the truck, the curator);
  • the weather: tonight's storm is seeded, so it can be forecast, and how many nights to the full moon and Crane's fair;
  • the radio's listings;
  • the classifieds: Madame N.'s hint, and somebody's lost tin box, buried, with where.

The Night Bus

At 3:19 in the morning a bus pulls up at every Night Bus Stop with somebody near it, and waits half a minute. One stop turns up near each of you on your first night out, and you can put more up yourself. Get on and pick where to: any other stop, Crane's fair (on a fair night) or the Starlight Annex (Night Service). It's dark on the bus, and the others on it don't move. One time in six it doesn't stop where you asked.

Dusk Delights, the ice cream truck

Some evenings (about one day in three), as it gets dark, its tune comes from somewhere off, and it drives up near you and parks. Its five treats cost 2 to 4 fair tickets each, and each has something odd about it (Treat Yourself). But some nights the tune slows and goes off-key, and its lights come on full: it's hunting, fast, and it hits hard. Get under a roof and stay there, and it gives up and drives away.

The seasons

By the real calendar, since 1.77.0:

  • Halloween (October 30 to November 1): the zombies and skeletons come out in carved pumpkins. Stand by a villager after dark and crouch (trick or treat) and they'll give you something: Candy Corn, a Wrapped Toffee and more. One toffee in five isn't what it seems (Trick or Treat).
  • The Longest Night (December 20 to 22): the night runs twice as long, and at midnight the Procession goes past, hooded Lantern Bearers walking slowly across the land, humming. Follow them to where they stop and they leave a Midwinter Lantern that never goes out (The Longest Night). Get in their way and they all stop, and turn, and look at you.

The clock

Since 1.77.4 the time is in the top-left corner of your screen: the world's clock (the hour and minute, as a clock on the wall says it) and which day it is, with a sun by day and a moon by night, in the colour of the sky at that hour. It hides with the HUD (F1, and in cutscenes), over the F3 screen, and on Tune-In Town's night shift, which keeps its own clock.
