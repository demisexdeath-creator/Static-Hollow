    Static Hollow tells most of its story in paper: journals on lecterns, logs in lockers, notices on walls. Every one is here word for word, grouped by chapter and by where you find it. They contain spoilers for everything up to the place they're found.

    Laboratory tapes and radio broadcasts are on Tapes and broadcasts. The Watcher's lines are on The Watcher speaks.

    Chapter one: the Nine

    The Forsaken Altar

    In the pilgrims' abandoned camp by the altar (since 1.40).

    Pilgrim's Diary

    M. Ashdown

    Found: the pilgrims' camp
    p. 1

    Day 9 on the road. We have reached the altar at last. Nine stones stand round it, one for each of the Nine, every one carved with one of their signs. We knelt at each of them.

    p. 2

    The candles at the altar lit themselves as we came near. Brother Tobias wept. He says it means we are welcome.

    p. 3

    Day 10. There is a stair by the altar, sealed under a stone. Tobias says it opens when the altar opens its eye. The first pilgrims are buried down there, all together, so they can go on praying.

    p. 4

    Day 12. Tobias has knelt at the altar three nights now. He does not come back to the fire. When I go to him he turns his head to me and smiles, and then he is not there.

    I am leaving in the morning. I will leave this for whoever comes after.

    The cathedral

    The priest's journal is in his study in the undercroft (since 1.40). The bell-ringer's tally is in the cathedral too.

    Journal

    Fr. A. Penn

    Found: the priest's study
    p. 1

    The Feast of the Nine.

    They came again tonight, the nine families, and sat in the front pews, and did not sing. When I asked them to go home they turned their heads to me.

    Only their heads.

    p. 2

    They do not eat. They do not go home. When I lock the doors at night they are already inside, praying, and the candles are lit that I did not light.

    p. 3

    Below, the Saint lies sealed in its tomb under nine chains and nine candles, one for each of the heads they took to make it. I have kept the candles burning forty years. I am so tired.

    p. 4

    The organ played itself at the third hour. A hymn I do not know. The candles went out one by one from the doors to the altar, as though someone were walking up the nave pinching them.

    p. 5

    The bell rang thirteen. There is no thirteen. Something stood in the doorway and did not come in.

    p. 6

    I went down this morning. The gate is bent outward. The tomb is open and empty and its chains are broken, and the congregation was smiling when I came back up.

    God keep whoever reads this. Do not open the tombs.

    Head Gardener's Journal

    E. Thorne

    Found: the Head Gardener's office
    p. 1

    The great flower opened today, under the dome. Nobody planted it. The roots came up through the tiles of the rotunda in a single night, and in the morning there it was - red as a wound, and warm to the touch.

    p. 2

    It is warm. I put my ear to the stalk and there is a beat in it, slow, like a sleeper's.

    The orchids have all turned to face it. The ferns too. I have begun to think the ferns are looking at things.

    p. 3

    The family came down from the house to see it. The children would not go near. The eldest said it smelled like the butcher's yard.

    They do not come any more. I have stopped going up to the house.

    p. 4

    Feeding. Bone meal will not do; it wants better. The rats are gone from the potting shed. The cat is gone.

    I do not remember the last time I ate. I am not hungry. I am never hungry now.

    p. 5

    The flies are thick on anyone who comes in now. Avery is black with them.

    They will not settle on me. It knows my clothes - it has grown into the cloth. My hat is in the shed, my coat in the chest here. Whoever wears them, it will take for me.

    p. 6

    There are roots in my boots. I cut them away in the morning and by evening they are back.

    They do not hurt. That is what frightens me. They do not hurt.

    p. 7

    Something came down from the house tonight - a great stitched thing, all iron and wrong pieces - and locked the gates from the outside, and took the key away with it.

    Good. Keep them out. Keep it in. Keep me in.

    p. 8

    I hear it say my name through the lilies. It says it needs tending.

    It is right. Somebody must tend it. I am going to kneel by it a while. Just a while.

    If you find this: do not wake me. Do not touch the flower.

    Chapter eight: the Dead Water

    The Watcher's chart

    The Watcher hands you his chart when you come down to the Dead Water (since 1.63.2). Unroll it and point at a water, and it shows what he wrote of it. These are his notes, in the chart's order.

    THE WATCHER'S CHART

    Found: in his hand, on the rig

    The Black Below. THE DEAD ZONE. The floor drops away and doesn't stop. The leviathans live down there. Don't.

    The Grey Shelf. Grey water over grey silt. The rig stands in it. Nothing here is in a hurry - mostly.

    The Haemal Reach. The water's thick with blood. Clotfish come in swarms; the Siphons wait for them to pass.

    The Wireweed. Weed like wire, and it sings. The Choir Eels nest in it. Don't touch the bright ones.

    The Lantern Fields. Lights in the dark as far as you can see. Most of them are bulbs. Some of them aren't.

    The Ossuary Flats. A plain of bone. Whatever died here, died big. The Stiltfins pick it clean.

    The Brine Sink. The brine pools eat what falls in. The water's sour, and the Puffbloats like it.

    The Molar Reef. Teeth, grown into a reef. The Grinners live in it. They smile at you.

    The Drowned Mycelia. Mushrooms the size of towers, drowned. Their spores burst if you brush them.

    The Rime Shelf. Ice under the sea. Things are frozen in it. Some of them wake up.

    The Ember Vents. Vents roaring up from below. Warm. The Mantlecrabs crowd round them - mind your power.

    The Carrier Wave. The static's loudest here. The water hums. Your instruments won't like it.

    Point at a water to see where it lies, and what he wrote of it.
