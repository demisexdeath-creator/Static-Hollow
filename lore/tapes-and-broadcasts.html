    Tapes and Broadcasts

    The Rosemont Botanical Research Laboratory's staff left six cassettes, and Shelter No. 4's radio set still picks up two broadcasts. Between them they tell you what happened under the gardens, what is asleep in the reactor pool, and how to get the Front Assembly out of Bunker B. Later, in Chapter Five, two things you carry start talking to you: a piece of the Chorus, and the one voice it couldn't swallow. In the Dead Water the sea itself has voices, Chapter Ten has a programme every night, and at Tune-In Town the old manager left a tape for each night of the shift.

    The Laboratory's audio logs

    Audio Log

    Use: play the tape, or put it in a tape recorder

    A cassette from the Laboratory, its label

    in somebody's hand. Someone was recording.

    Use a tape to play it: the click, the hiss, and the voice, line by line. Since 1.41 every line is voiced, slowed and dropped an octave into something too big for the room, saturated and tape-worn, catching on a syllable now and then. Each tape ends with it laughing. Subtitles show over your hotbar while it plays, and the words go into chat too.

    Put a tape in a Tape Recorder and it plays out loud for anyone within about twenty blocks: its reels turn, its level meter jumps and static crackles off its speaker. Sneak and use it to take the tape out. The Laboratory's recorders still have their tapes in them. Researchers sometimes drop the later tapes.

    Audio Log: Marsh, Control Room

    *click* - Dr. H. Marsh

    This is Marsh. Control room. The power's gone, and the Core is all that's keeping it asleep.

    Specimen Zero went into the pool the night the tank burst. It sleeps against the Core. It likes the warmth.

    If anyone needs the Core out: the coolant has to be isolated first, or the hall stays sealed. The levers are on this wall.

    The order matters. [the lever order, different in every world]. Any other order and the whole level alarms.

    Then the blast doors open. Then you lift it. Then you run, because it will wake.

    I'm going down to sit with it. Somebody should.

    [something on the tape laughs]

    Pull the three coolant levers in Marsh's order and the hall's blast doors grind up ("COOLANT ISOLATED - REACTOR HALL OPENING"). Pull one out of turn and the alarm goes ("WRONG SEQUENCE - COOLANT ISOLATION FAULT"), the levers drop back, and six of the staff come for you.

    It hates the flowers. Every sample we put near it, it reached for. Tore at.

    The keycard's in this drawer. The tank won't hold much longer.

    [something on the tape laughs]

    Audio Log: Okafor, Sample Log

    Flower ash kills it outright. Burn the Gardens' flowers, pack the ash with powder, and you have something it can't stand.

    Shelter No. 4's radio

    Use the Shelter Radio Set in the radio room to hear it. With the power off it plays the evacuation notice. With both generators running, it turns into Control's emergency broadcast.

    The evacuation notice

    Rosemont Civil Defence, on a loop

    This is Rosemont Civil Defence. This is not a drill.

    All residents of zones C and D proceed at once to Shelter Number Four, Victoria Park.

    Bring nothing. Do not stop for anyone on the street who is unwell.

    Wardens will close the blast door at twenty-two hundred hours. It will not be opened again until the all-clear.

    This is Rosemont Civil Defence. This is not a drill.

    Control's emergency broadcast

    Once the shelter's power is back

    Shelter Four, Shelter Four, this is Control. We see your power on. Respond.

    Shelter Four, do not open the vault. Repeat: do not open the vault.

    Whatever is in bunk hall B is not your residents any more. Whatever the squad told you, they are not coming back up.

    If anyone down there can hear this - the overload switch, over the plates in bunker B. Get it onto the plates.

    Shelter Four, respond.

    That's how to beat the Warden of Shelter No. 4.

    Voices from the static

    These aren't recordings. They're things that are still alive enough to talk, and what they say comes up over your hotbar in italics when you hold them to your ear.

    The Chorus Fragment

    "A piece the Chorus left behind as it went down. It's still part of it, wherever it is." It's a knot of grey-violet flesh with a face in it the size of a thumbnail, its eyes shut, its mouth moving. You get one when the Chorus runs from its pit. Hold it to your ear and it whispers what the Chorus is thinking, down there:

    One at random

    down. down where it is warm.

    we remember the dark under the hills.

    we are not alone down here. there are others. older.

    the bloom's roots go deeper than you ever dug.

    come down and find us. we are learning your name.

    you broke our thoughts. we are growing new ones.

    listen. under your feet. that is us.

    every one we take remembers. every one remembers you.

    Since 1.54.0, while the Chorus is still alive it also tells you which way it went. Face the right way and it whispers clearly, and what it says tells you how far. Side on, it's faint and broken. Facing away, there's only static.

    Up top, leading you to the Sinkhole: far (over 600 blocks), that way, near, here

    down. a long way down. far from here - that way.

    that way. under the hills. we can feel you coming.

    closer. we can hear you walking. we can hear your heart.

    here. right under you. come down. it's warm.

    Coming out in the Deep

    home. we are home. come and find us. we are deeper.

    In the Deep, leading you to the Warm Heart

    deeper. a long way yet. through the dark - that way.

    that way. past the old ones' halls. they don't like us. they won't like you.

    close now. can you hear us singing? we can hear you.

    here. we are right here. we are finished growing.

    Side on, or facing away

    ...colder... no... turn...

    (nothing but static)

    What the Chorus says out loud, through its forms and from its pit, is on The Watcher speaks, with his answers. Since 1.60.3 the Chorus also has a hidden voice: now and then, out of all its faces muttering together, one voice speaks on its own for fourteen seconds while the rest hush. It's a sound with a subtitle ("The Chorus: one voice out of all of them"), not words on the screen.

    The Last Voice

    "A face cut out of the Chorus, its eyes open now. It isn't singing. It's trying to tell you something: a door at the end of the world, and the seal on it." It comes out of the Chorus, whole, when it dies in the Warm Heart. Hold it up and it talks:

    One at random

    "...there's a door. at the end. he sealed it..."

    "...it was so loud in there. it's so quiet now..."

    "...take me to the end of the world. i'll open it for you..."

    "...i was a farmer. i think. i had a dog..."

    "...the one in the black coat knows. ask him what he locked up..."

    "...don't put me down. please don't put me down in the dark again..."

    In the End

    "...here. it's here. can you feel it? something on the other side is listening..."

    "...hold me up. here. he made this seal with his own hands. i can open it..."

    Something cracks, all the way across the End's sky - and stays cracked. The seal he put on it is broken.

    "...there. out past the obsidian. a door. go through it. i'll be quiet now..."

    In the Stalls

    "...shh. don't wake them. they're watching..."

    "...they're getting up. all of them..."

    It said it would be quiet, but if you're still carrying it, it whispers in the Stalls too: when you first come in, and when the Audience gets up for the Crowd. See Chapter Six.

    Voices in the Dead Water

    Down in the Dead Water (1.63.9 to 1.63.16), things talk to you that shouldn't be able to. Most of it comes up over your hotbar, in italics; a few voices come in chat. "[your name]" is where they say yours.

    The Grove

    Every so often the whole of the Drowned Mycelia lets go its spores at once, and for a minute they come down through the water like snow. In sporefall you can hear the Grove. You hear it too once its spores have taken root in you (spore sickness at 50 and over), whispering in your ear, in pale violet. It goes through its lines in turn:

    The spores are falling...

    ...lie down. ...the floor is soft here...

    ...we grew over the others. ...we'll grow over you, [your name]...

    ...breathe in. ...it only hurts the first time...

    ...everything that sinks, we keep...

    ...you can hear us now. ...that's the spores, in you...

    ...don't struggle. ...the threads only pull tighter...

    ...we were divers too, once...

    ...the caps remember your face, [your name]...

    ...rest. ...rest. ...rest...

    ...the Grove is so hungry, [your name]...

    And what spore sickness says about you, as it builds up: "Your skin itches. Something small is coming up on your shoulders." "You cough, and spores come out of you." "The spores have taken root. They won't leave you on their own now." "It's in your lungs. Your arms are heavy in the water." "The Grove has you."

    The Carrier Wave's broadcast

    Now and then in the Carrier Wave a tune like a music box plays out of every horn in the water at once, and then a voice reads out numbers, three of them, twice. There are no words: the numbers are a place, the spot where somebody's left a dead drop (a chest of supplies, a horn on a post beside it), and they show over your hotbar as they're read. A Receiver carried while it plays keeps them ("Your receiver crackles: it caught the numbers."), and the echo jellies play them back for days.

    Tune-In Town

    The night shift at Tune-In Town Pizza & Playhouse is a secret that can turn up any time after your first day, with flashing lights, loud noises and jumpscares (read the photosensitivity warning). The flyer that starts it: "Tune-In Town Pizza & Playhouse: Night Security, 12AM-6AM. Apply within. (It was on the television.)"

    Hal's orientation tapes

    Each night the office TV plays the orientation tape: Hal, the old manager. Since 1.71.2 every line is spoken in his voice, off the tape (warbling, hissing; the last nights' wrecked), with the subtitles below. Switch the TV off and he's cut off mid-word. Night six's announcer, and the voice that isn't Hal's, are their own.

    Night one

    Hey, hey! Welcome to Tune-In Town - and welcome to the night shift. This is Hal. I manage the place. Managed. You'll see.

    First things first: the cast. After hours they're on free-roam - keeps the servos from locking up. If one comes to the office, it'll want to say hello. Don't let it.

    Two doors, and the vent behind you. Red buttons shut them; white ones light the hall outside. The lights are how you see who's there.

    Everything costs power - the doors, the lights, the cameras. The generator's old. If it runs out... just don't let it run out.

    The music box in the Prize Corner - keep it wound. You can wind it from camera eight. Jack doesn't like the quiet.

    Oh - and if the TV in your office comes on by itself, turn it off. Quick. Don't ask me why. I don't know why.

    That's night one. Six o'clock and you're done. Goodnight, from all of us here at Channel 6.

    Night two

    Rover's back behind the Pit Stop curtain. OUT OF ORDER. Check on him on camera three - he hates being ignored. When he comes out, he runs.

    Bucky's taken to chewing on the shutters. Doesn't hurt the door - eats your power, though. Funny thing to program into a beaver.

    If you hear knocking at one door, check the other. Lulu does voices. She does knocks, too.

    One more thing. Some nights one of them comes in... wrong. Twitchy. Its eyes go red. The lights go funny when it's close, and you'll hear it breathing behind the door. And the door won't stop it. Nothing will.

    If that happens, pull up the monitor and hit REBOOT - R - on whichever one it is. Pick the right one. Pick the wrong one and you've burned power for nothing, and it locks you out a while.

    Anyway. Doors shut, lights on, eyes open. See you tomorrow.
