    What it is

    A dead green sea that goes on forever, a grey sky and a grey fog over it, and a black sea under that. Standing alone in it is the rig the Watcher built long ago "for when i needed somewhere to hide". You come down onto its helipad out of the portal he tore open as you fell from the Hub. That earns Dead Water, and the title reads CHAPTER EIGHT / DEAD WATER / the deep is listening.

    Then he goes over the rail. The whole arrival is on Chapter Eight; what the sea does after that is Chapter Nine.

    Stranded: the Broken Remote

    The Remote he gives you cracks in your hand: the Broken Remote. "Chapter Eight: Dead Water. The Remote's broken - mend it with a Static Leviathan's Signal Organ and a copper ingot. They live deep."

    • Until it's mended you're held here. Die and "The sea brings you back. Nothing leaves the Dead Water while the Remote's broken." Without one of the rig's beds you wake in its quarters. You can't lose the Broken Remote either: "The Broken Remote's still in your pocket."
    • Mending it: the Broken Remote, a Signal Organ from a Static Leviathan, and a copper ingot. Go more than forty blocks down in the water (anywhere but the Black Below) and now and then one of them comes up out of the dark after you, singing. Mending it earns Back On Air.
    • Mended: "The Remote works again. Press it: the Hub (and in the Hub, home). Crouch and press it: back to the rig."
    • Mutated: at the end of Chapter Nine the Static Heart (Mutated) goes into the Remote, broken or mended, and makes the Remote (Mutated). It goes to the same places, and it has the Undertow's roar in it. See Chapter Nine.

    The Watcher also presses his chart into your hands when you come down. Held, it shows the stretch of sea you're in, a thousand blocks to a square, with you, the others down there with you, and the rig. Use it to unroll the whole chart: since 1.63.5 the whole sea, out past the edge, every water in its own colour and named, the floor's depths shaded, and the Black Below hatched out in red as THE DEAD ZONE. Since 1.63.6 every place in it has a red cross and its name. Point at a water and you read his note on it. Scroll to zoom, drag to look round, R to come back to you.

    One sea: how it's laid out

    Since 1.63.5 the Dead Water is one sea, laid out the same way in every world. The rig stands in the middle of the Grey Shelf, about 420 blocks of it all round. Round that lies a ring of the five shallow waters, out to about 1,500 blocks, one of each; round that a ring of the five deeper waters, set between them, out to about 2,950. Their edges wander, and the floor slopes from one water to the next. About 3,000 blocks out the floor slopes down to the edge of everything and falls away: the Black Below, the void, all the way round, for ever.

    Since 1.63.6 each water has one place of its own, rebuilt bigger, to go into (they used to come up all over their waters, again and again). Each stands in the middle of its own water's stretch of the map:

    "Which way" is the middle of each water; the distances are to where each place is meant to stand. A place takes the flattest floor near its spot that's all its own water, so in your world it may stand a little off. The first ids are the old ones, kept: the habitat dome is Lantern Station now, the radio tower the Wireweed Mast, and so on. The Drowned Heart is about four hundred blocks west of the Haemal Research Station, as the station's log says (put right in 1.63.18): "The Heart's four hundred metres off our west lock".

    • Finding them. /locate structure finds each place from anywhere in the Dead Water. Since 1.63.18 it sets you down in the water right beside it, a third of the way up it, where you can see it.
    • Level ground. Since 1.63.21 the floor round each place is level with its own floor out to 24 blocks past it: hills that ran into it cut away, holes and trenches beside it filled, rock spikes, giant mushrooms, wireweed and bergs taken off, and the ground eased back to the floor as it was at the edge. It's done the first time you come near each one, in older worlds too (chests are left be).
    • Old worlds. A world that had already been out there keeps the sea it had seen, and its places where they were; everywhere still new is the new sea. Since 1.63.11 any place an old world is missing is built the first time you come near.

    The rig

    An oil platform on four great legs of rusted steel lattice that go down into the black to the sea floor, braced every fourteen blocks. It was waiting with its lights on and its larders full. The derrick stands in the middle, a lattice tower with red lights at its crown and its drill string going down into the moon pool's dark (since 1.63.22 its ladder has every rung, so you can climb it). There are two cranes, the flare stack out over the sea on the north that never goes out, and lifeboats on the west. Railings run round everything and stairs go between the decks. On the rig the music is The Rig.

    The cellar deck

    Eight blocks over the sea.

    • The moon pool: a square of open water in the middle, a ladder down into it and a winch over it, a heavy weight on its chain.
    • The dive locker (north-west): racks of gear, a shower and chests with Air Tanks, Marine Flares, Harpoon Bolts, a Harpoon Gun, a turtle shell and potions.
    • The pumps (north-east).
    • The pens (south-west): chickens, sheep, cows and a pig, let out when you arrive, with feed. Since 1.63.25 the hay and the trough are in the middle of each pen, so nothing steps over the fence.
    • The boat dock below it on the south, with two boats.

    The main deck

    • The quarters: eight beds, and chests with rations, a clock, a compass, candles and some leather clothes. If you die without a bed of your own while the Remote's broken, this is where the sea brings you back.
    • The mess and its kitchen: cooked fish, bread, stew, pie and Rig Rations.
    • The infirmary (west): golden apples, potions, milk and honey, syringes and pill bottles.
    • The workshop (east): copper, iron, glass, gunpowder, pistons and redstone; and a tool chest with iron tools, a fishing rod, shears, a shield and a spyglass.
    • The stores (east): food, coal, torches, string, leads, ladders, planks and wool, and a boat.
    • The greenhouse (east): crops growing under glass, water channels between the rows, a composter, and a chest of seeds and bone meal.

    The upper deck

    • The control room (north): consoles, windows all round, and a chest with a map, a compass, a recovery compass and flares.
    • The radio room (middle): the set, its mast up through the roof.
    • The armoury (south): Harpoon Guns and their bolts, iron armour, a sword, a bow and crossbow, a shield, flares and flashbangs.
    • The helipad, over the east side: where you come down.

    Every door's buttons are on the wall beside it (since 1.63.2), the cranes' hooks are iron blocks that stay on their chains, and the greenhouse's channels have a bottom (1.63.22-24). A rig set down before each fix mends itself the next time you're on it, without touching anything you built.

    Under the rig: the Watcher, asleep

    Since 1.63.21, straight down from where he went into the sea off the rig, the Watcher is there at the bottom: curled up, asleep, floating in a bubble of his own static with a faint light round him in the dark. Nothing gets into the bubble (it gives a little, then pushes you back out), and nothing you swing, throw or fire at it gets through. He doesn't wake, until the Turning breaks his bubble and he comes up onto the helipad. See the Watcher.

    The sub bay and Orpheus

    Off the south end of the dock is the sub bay: a berth of open water between two steel catwalks, open to the sea on the south. The docking clamps are on a cradle under its water, with a gantry and lamps over it and a barrel of spares (copper, iron, glass, redstone, an Air Tank) by the way in. Since 1.63.8 a parts crate stands on the catwalk too, with a Depth Module in it, iron and a block of redstone. In its clamps lies the submarine Orpheus, and its clamp charges her power and mends her hull. Rigs already standing before 1.63.0 get the bay added.

    Orpheus is thirteen blocks long with a cabin you walk about in: the helm, floodlights and sonar at the bow, and the chart table, bunks, lockers, workbench, galley, battery bank, repair board and motor down her length. Since 1.63.22 her cabin is dry, clear air, with the sea starting at her glass. Come down through her hatch, or crouch and use her, to climb in; hold crouch to climb out, onto the catwalk in the bay or into the water at sea. How to drive her is on the items page.

    Her upgrade bay

    Use her motor (aft, in the engine room) and her upgrade bay opens: five mounts for modules, to fit, swap and take out as you like. What's in it is what she has, and the motor tells you her rating.

    • Nothing fitted: she's rated for 183 metres (down to y -120). Past that the hull creaks and gives, the dome cracks, and at nothing she implodes.
    • The Depth Module (280 m): rated to 280 metres. One is in the parts crate, and it can be made from iron blocks, copper blocks, tinted glass and a block of redstone.
    • Pressure Plating: no crush depth at all.
    • The UV Field: her floodlights burn violet and keep Enzyme 1-2418 off her, and burn it out of her crew.

    Once she has all three of those modules in her bay, the Turning plays (Chapter Nine).

    The bay's console

    Since 1.63.21 the console on the catwalk by her berth tells you where she is: in her clamps (her hull and her power), out at sea (where she was last heard from, and how far), or nothing heard from her at all. Crushed, eaten or sunk, the bay's welders build her again in her clamps: a minute's work, nothing asked of you. Lost out of reach (stuck somewhere, sunk too deep)? Use the console crouching: she's scrapped where she lies and built again. Its screen shows her sonar, the welders at work, or red, NO SIGNAL.

    Her emergency locker

    Since 1.66.6, when she's lost (crushed, swallowed, gone to flesh, or scrapped from the console) her emergency locker blows free with everything that was in her lockers and the modules out of her bay. It shoots up to the top of the sea (to the nearest open water if she went down under ice or rock) and floats there, its lamp blinking and a flare going up now and then. Her crew, and anyone else in the sea, are told where it came up and how far off it is, and it's on your locator bar as a little yellow locker however far away you are. Use it to open it (36 slots); empty it and it sinks away.

    Who lives down there

    Since 1.63.7 the places have people of their own: the Deep Hands, who still live and work down there in the old diving dress (a round copper helmet with a face behind its lit glass, a brass corselet, lead boots, an air hose, a big spanner). Each working place has its own crew in its own colours. They walk from job to job, look round at you, wave when you first come near, and never go for you (the Salvagers aside). Right-click one and it talks to you (each crew has eight things to say). Hurt one and it backs off, and so does the rest of its crew. They don't drown, and the vents and the brine don't hurt them.

    Two places have lost their crews to what they found. The Drowned went down to see what the Heart was, and stayed: their dress split, the Flesh coming out through it, their glass cracked and full of red, two lights in it. Five walk the Heart's floor and one waits inside it. The Sporeborn are the same, taken by the Grove's spores, and since 1.63.11 more of them hang up under the Grove's caps on strings. The other places are left to the sea: hookjaws and clotfish at the Jaw, hookjaws and mantlecrabs on the Leviathan Fall, frostjaws round the Rime Outpost and the Spires. The Anchor is left to the Undertow.

    They're set out the first time you come near a place and made good slowly after (a crew in about five minutes, the Drowned and the rest in a day). All of them are in the bestiary.

    The deep crushes you

    Since 1.63.10, below about y -100 in the Dead Water the sea crushes you, more the deeper you go: you hear yourself creak, you slow down, and deeper still the world swims. Each piece of the Ember Deep Suit keeps a quarter of it off, and Orpheus keeps it off her crew. See survival.

    The twelve waters

    Each water has its own colour and murk, its own floor, plants, life, light, sound and music, and its own fish (since 1.63.11). Glowing plankton drifts in every one in its colour, flaring up in your wake. The sea's sound changes as you swim from one to the next, and the reverb follows the water: longer and darker the deeper you go. Drowned and glow squid turn up in most of them. From 1.63.12 to 1.63.17 nine of the waters (all but the Haemal Reach, the Ember Vents and the Black Below) were filled in with new growth, new creatures, an event of its own that comes now and then, and things to make; in an older world they grow in a little at a time, out past where you can see.

    "His note" is what the Watcher wrote of each water on his chart. The creatures are in the bestiary and the things made from them on the items page.

    The Grey Shelf

    His note: "Grey water over grey silt. The rig stands in it. Nothing here is in a hurry - mostly."

    The rig's own grey-green water, over ash silt. Grey kelp and a few glow anemones grow here. Gentle Pipeyes, Lanternlets, Hum Skates, Stiltfins and Hymnbacks live here, with Hookjaws, Siphons and Needlemaws among them, and the Mouth of the Floor lies buried in the silt. Its fish is the Staticfin. You hear something groan, far off.

    Since 1.63.17 ghost nets stand up off the floor in great curtains, held up by floats and pinned down by sunk girders, with bones and skulls still hanging in them (swim into the mesh and it holds you). Between them grow drowned hair that tangles you, oil seeps that blind you, net floats on their tethers, and girders hung with rusticles. Since 1.63.18 all of it comes in to the feet of the rig's legs, the ghost nets to sixty blocks off. The Catch rolls over the floor (a ball of net with the drowned rolled up in it, groping), and crowds of little Buoylings live under the net floats.

    The Diving Bell: now and then a ship's horn sounds somewhere up above and a chain runs out. An old diving bell comes down out of the murk near you, its hatch swings open, and its supplies are set out beside it in a chest (sometimes the diver's still down there with it). After a while it's hauled back up.

    Its place: the Lifeboat Graveyard. The rig's launching davit has fallen over, one boat still hanging off it in its falls, with lifeboats strewn round it: one upright you can climb into (its benches, its flare locker), one split open, one on its back, one nose-down in the silt; a raft; a buoy on its chain, its lamp still lit. The Castaways live round it.

    The Haemal Reach

    His note: "The water's thick with blood. Clotfish come in swarms; the Siphons wait for them to pass."

    Blood-red water, thick as soup and lit red, over clot silt. Vein kelp (it glows), artery anemones and clot coral grow here. Pipeyes, Grinners, Puffbloats, Clotfish and Siphons live here, and its fish is the Bloodlet, which smells blood a long way off.

    Enzyme 1-2418 is in all of its water (since 1.63.9). Go in without the UV dress and it gets into you, out of a hundred. At 35 what you're wearing grows into you and won't come off; at 50 it wants to go home, pulling you toward the Drowned Heart; at 100 (or 80 near the Heart) it takes you there, into itself. A UV Lamp flash burns a fifth of it out, an Enzyme Inhibitor half, and the whole UV Dive Suit keeps it out. Orpheus without her UV Field goes to flesh in it. The detail is on survival.

    The Drowned Heart: a heart the size of a church. Its four chambers are hollow and joined by their open valves: swim in by the wound in its side or down its torn aorta, the veins glowing in its walls, its arteries branching away over the floor. Something is kept in its right atrium. Since 1.63.18 it beats once every forty seconds, a slow double thump you can hear a long way off and follow; dark blood shakes off it with each beat, and up close the water shoves at you. The Drowned keep it.

    Haemal Research Station

    At the Reach's near edge, a quick swim from the Grey Shelf: the lab where Enzyme 1-2418 was made, and where it got out. White modules round a domed hub, the dome caved in. The specimen lab's containment vat has burst and the flesh has poured out of it, up every wall, through the hub and out of a breach in its side. The office has the station's last log book, Research Log, HRS-3 ("Haemal Research Station's last log book, in its sealed sleeve"), and the inhibitors. The decontamination room, its UV strips still lit, is where the flesh never got in (it's burnt black in the doorway), and in its lockers is the UV Dive Suit. The station's divers lie where it left them, stretched out long. Some of them are only lying: the Stretched, who unfold when you come near and come fast when you look away.

    The Wireweed

    His note: "Weed like wire, and it sings. The Choir Eels nest in it. Don't touch the bright ones."

    Dark green water over rust silt, a forest of wireweed and signal bulbs. Choir Eels, Hookjaws, Pipeyes, Lanternlets, Hum Skates and Grinners live here, and the wires sing. Its fish is the Wirewhip, an eel of old cable with a plug for a head.

    Since 1.63.15 it's a forest of cable trees (black cable braided thick, hanging wire coming down off their limbs) and toppled pylons of rusted girder, with copper moss, insulator clusters and fuse pods (touch one and it blows) on the floor. Wirecutters wait in the roots; Coilbacks graze the copper moss. The Power Surge: now and then a hum, sparks running along the cable, and then the current comes, shocking anything touching the cable, the wire, the moss or the insulators until it's over.

    Its place: the Wireweed Mast, a lattice mast fifty blocks high with a ladder up one leg, two rest platforms, its dish and its red light blinking at the top; the operator's hut at its foot (his radios, his bunk, his logs); a second mast fallen across the floor, its guy wires, and the weed grown up through all of it. The Signal Keepers work it.

    The Lantern Fields

    His note: "Lights in the dark as far as you can see. Most of them are bulbs. Some of them aren't."

    Black-blue, clear water lit blue by its plants, over glow sand. Glow anemones and lantern bulbs grow here. Lanternlets, Thimble Jellies and Hymnbacks live here, and so do Gulpers with their lures. Glass chimes. Its fish is the Wickling, a stub of wax with a flame on its wick.

    Since 1.63.15 lantern trees of white wax coral stand here, hung with old iron lanterns on ropes, candle coral burning round their feet; rows of lantern buoys float up off the floor; lure stalks, glow moss and drowned lanterns, still lit, stand about. Lantern Moths go about in clouds and come to any light, and rarely one grows into a Lampmother, a moth the size of a boat. The Blackout: now and then the lanterns all round you flicker and go out at once, and in the dark something comes looking for you. After a while they come back on, one by one.

    Its place: Lantern Station, a seabase. Its glass-domed mess hall; a corridor west to its lab (benches, the specimen tanks still glowing); a corridor east to its moon pool (lockers, the pool down through its floor); a corridor north to its tower, a ladder up to the lookout bubble; lamp posts out over the fields. Its Keepers live there.

    The Ossuary Flats

    His note: "A plain of bone. Whatever died here, died big. The Stiltfins pick it clean."

    Milky water over a floor of bone sand, with bone coral, bone fans and marrow sponge. Stiltfins, Pipeyes, Hum Skates, Hookjaws and Gulpers live here, the Mouth of the Floor lies buried here too, and bones knock. Its fish is the Marrowpike, which lies among the bones as if it's one of them.

    Since 1.63.16 whale falls lie half sunk in the sand, red bone worms all over them and carcass mat spread round them, with vertebrae and ribs standing up between. The Sleeper, a blind shark as long as a boat, smells you when you're hurt and comes slowly, and doesn't stop; colonies of Skullkeeps (crabs in human skulls) pick over the falls. The Fall: now and then a groan far up, a darkness coming down, and a dead whale lands not far off in a cloud of bone sand, its flesh rotting off a piece at a time. The Sleeper and the skullkeeps come to it.

    Its place: the Leviathan Fall, the skeleton of something ninety blocks long: its ribs arching up into a hall to swim the length of, its skull a cave with its eye sockets open (something left inside), its jaw fallen open in front. Hookjaws pick over it and mantlecrabs sit on its bones.

    The Brine Sink

    His note: "The brine pools eat what falls in. The water's sour, and the Puffbloats like it."

    Sick yellow-green water over brine crust, with sulfur tubes. Puffbloats, Thimble Jellies, Clotfish and Siphons live here, and the brine fizzes. Its fish is the Brinecrust, buried in the floor until you step on it.

    Since 1.63.14 its brine pools are lakes on the sea floor with a surface of their own: what swims into them is eaten. Round their shores are lips of salt crust, mussel beds packed black, pink halite spires, cold seeps breathing methane, and fish pickled where they fell. Slimehags tangle where things are dead, and colonies of Wavers (white yeti crabs) all stop, face you and wave, in step. The Brine Surge: the floor rumbles and a pool, or the floor, erupts in a boiling torrent of bubbles and brine.

    Its place: Sink Station, two huts on stilts over the brine lake joined by a catwalk (the lab and the winch house), its crane out over the lake, its sampling cage still down in the brine, and the frame of a third hut, gone. Its Researchers work there.

    The Molar Reef

    His note: "Teeth, grown into a reef. The Grinners live in it. They smile at you."

    Teal and pink water over gum silt: a reef of enamel coral, tooth fans, gum sponge and nerve anemones. Grinners, Puffbloats, Lanternlets, Clotfish and Hookjaws live here, the Mouth of the Floor lies buried here too, and teeth chatter. Its fish is the Gumnibbler, which nibbles at you if you keep still.

    Since 1.63.17 giant teeth as tall as towers stand up out of mounds of gum flesh. Some have gone bad, black decay eating down one side and a cavity big enough to swim into, with something left at the back. Milk teeth, tongue weed, plaque and gum blisters (they burst in a sour cloud and leave Reef Gum) are on the floor. The Tooth Fairy takes something off your hotbar and leaves a gold nugget; the Gnasher grinds your armour down. The Eruption: a toothache (your head throbs, your hands go slow), then the reef teethes, new teeth pushing up through the gum a layer at a time. Chew Reef Gum and the ache goes.

    Its place: the Jaw, a vault of gum and teeth to swim through: incisors and canines hanging over the way in, molars at the back, teeth standing along the floor, a tongue; a canine fallen outside; and a rotten molar with something hidden in its hollow.

    The Drowned Mycelia

    His note: "Mushrooms the size of towers, drowned. Their spores burst if you brush them."

    Violet water with spores in it, over spore silt. Gill glow and spore puffs grow here (they burst in your face). Pipeyes, Thimble Jellies, Gulpers and Siphons live here, and the drowned whisper. Its fish is the Sporegill.

    Since 1.63.12 giant drowned mushrooms stand all over its floor (parasols, bulbs, clusters, old ones fallen), their gills glowing, hyphae hanging down out of them in long strands that cling to you, shelf fungus up their stems to stand on. On the floor: hyphae mats, lantern caps, dead man's fingers, and ear fungus that whispers. Spore Drifters drift in shoals, and a Gillmaw is a mushroom on the floor until its cap splits open with teeth in it. Now and then one of the rooted dead lies grown over on the floor, until strings come down out of the dark and haul it up. Sporefall: every so often the whole Mycelia lets its spores go at once, and for a minute they come down like snow.

    Spore sickness (1.63.13): breathe its water and its spores build up in you. At 50 they've taken root and won't fade by themselves down here; at 100 the Grove has you. A sealed helmet keeps most of it out, Orpheus all of it, and a Spore Tonic burns sixty of it out of you. Milk does nothing. See survival.

    Its caves and the Gillmaws' nests

    Sinkholes in its floor, their rims matted and hyphae hanging down into them, open into winding fungal tunnels: glowing gills and shelf fungus on the walls, hyphae curtains, the rooted dead lying in them. Some come back up to the floor somewhere else. Some open into a Gillmaw nest, a wider hollow floored with bones and skulls, Gillmaws waiting in it, and a drowned diver's barrel.

    Its place: the Drowned Grove, a mushroom forty blocks high. Go in at its foot and climb the ladder up inside its stem to the camp someone made under its glowing cap; a grove of smaller ones stands round it, one fallen. The Sporeborn walk it, more hang under its caps on their strings (swim under one and it drops on you), and a gulper waits in the canopy.

    The Grove Heart

    Since 1.63.13 the great mushroom is hollow, and its hollow goes on down, through the floor, twenty blocks, into a hollow in the dark: roots as thick as a man in out of its walls, curtains of hyphae, the rooted dead on its floor, and in the middle, on its root mass, the Grove Heart. A mass of fungal flesh the size of a room, breathing, its veins lit, its strings running up out of it into the dark: the strings the Sporeborn hang on. It knows you're there, and it talks to you: "...so. ...you found your way down to us..." You can't fight it, not yet. Hit it and "It doesn't feel that. The Grove does." The Grove answers: spores in your face, the rooted dead getting up, the hung ones coming down for you. Near it, the spores take you fast. In the Turning the Undertow comes down through the roof of the hollow and eats it.

    The Rime Shelf

    His note: "Ice under the sea. Things are frozen in it. Some of them wake up."

    Cold, clear water under a roof of rime ice, over rime silt, with frost anemones and frozen corpses. Packs of Frostjaws hunt here, and Stiltfins and Hymnbacks live here too. The ice groans. Its fish is the Rime Smelt, clear ice with a little red heart showing through.

    Since 1.63.14 sunken floes the size of a house lie tilted on the floor, fish (and now and then someone) frozen in them, anchor ice grown under their edges, and brinicles stand round them. Rime Striders wade the floor and Rimebacks call in long electric trills. Brinicles growing: a crackling above you, and a thread of ice comes down to the floor; when it touches, the cold spreads out and freezes whatever's caught in it nearly still.

    Its places: the Rime Outpost, its cabin and store shed locked in ice (bunks, stove, stores), its radio mast, a sledge still loaded, its fire out, its people; the Rime Crew have their camp by it. And the Rime Spires, a cluster of spires round a great one fifty blocks high, veined with blue ice, an arch of ice between two, and sealed in clear ice at the great one's heart, lit, someone, and what they had with them.

    The Ember Vents

    His note: "Vents roaring up from below. Warm. The Mantlecrabs crowd round them - mind your power."

    Rust-orange water lit orange, over ember crust, with smoking vent chimneys and tube worms. Mantlecrabs (their pulse kills every light near them, the submarine's too), Choir Eels, Hum Skates, Stiltfins and Needlemaws live here, and the vents roar. Its fish is the Cinderling, a live coal that burns you.

    Ember Pumping Station

    Its place, rebuilt in 1.63.10. The portal hall: a Nether portal in the sea floor, sealed under an iron bulkhead, the lava main coming up out of it into the pumps, and a conveyor carrying the netherite debris it brings up to a hopper and a chest. The pump house: its engine, its great flywheel, its control board, two lava tanks glowing. The intakes drawing the sea in; the outflow pipe dumping the spent lava in a heap of obsidian and basalt; the workshop; the pipeline out on its supports to the wellhead capping the great vent, its stack; a gantry crane over the vent field. The Ember Crew work all of it.

    Its trade: right-click one of the Ember Crew (crouch to just talk) and they trade iron, copper and gold for Ember Plating, Heat Coils, Pressure Seals and Vent Glass (and ancient debris, now and then). They buy Needle Teeth, Shroud Hide, Photophores and Signal Organs, and restock every half day. What they trade makes the Ember Deep Suit, the strongest suit there is: see the items page.

    The Carrier Wave

    His note: "The static's loudest here. The water hums. Your instruments won't like it."

    Blue-grey water with static shimmering through it, over static sand. Static coral, antenna weed and signal bulbs grow here, and drowned television sets hiss on the floor. Choir Eels, Mantlecrabs, Pipeyes, Lanternlets and Hum Skates live here, and there's a voice in the static. Its fish is the Dialfin, old bakelite with a lit radio dial on each flank.

    Since 1.63.16 great listening dishes stand on their pedestals tilted up at a surface nobody can see, and old loudspeaker horns hiss on posts. Tape weed, tuning fork coral, speaker anemones and valve bulbs grow between. The Handset lies on the floor ringing, an old telephone handset for its lure; Echo Jellies play back what they've heard. The Broadcast: a music-box tune out of every horn in the water at once, then a voice reading out three numbers, twice. They're a place, and someone's left a dead drop there on the floor, a chest of supplies with a horn on a post beside it. (The numbers show over your hotbar; a Receiver keeps them.)

    Its place: the Wreck Field, a cargo ship broken in two. Its stern half sits upright with its bridge house standing (three decks: its bridge, its cabins, its funnel and mast light); its bow half lies off on its own, its hold open; its containers are spilled, one full of televisions still showing static; and a plane is nose-down in the floor. The Salvagers are cutting it all up.

    The Black Below

    His note: "THE DEAD ZONE. Past the edge of all this the floor drops away and doesn't stop. Something lives out there that's bigger than the sea. Don't."

    Past the edge, all the way round: the void. Near-black water, barely any light, over abyss silt with void moss and eye pods on it (shut until you come near; then the eye opens and follows you). The chart's tooltip says it plainly: "The black hatched in red is the dead zone. Don't take the sub over it." There's no music here, only the sound of the ocean. Its fish is the Omenfry: black specks with human eyes, lit, in clouds, that trail you at the edge of your light and stare.

    The void is the Undertow's. Go out over it at all (swimming, in Orpheus, in a boat on top of it) and stay, and it comes, sooner the deeper you are. Get back over the edge and it lets you go. Nothing else comes up out of the Black Below. Within ten seconds or so of you going out over it a lost whale comes out of the dark ahead of you, singing, its back glowing warm, and when the Undertow comes it goes for the whale first.

    The Canticle's Anchor

    Its place, out past the edge (rebuilt in 1.63.8): the anchor of the SS Canticle, a hundred blocks of it on the floor of the abyss. Its shank; its stock lying across its head; its crown, hollow (go in on its west side); one arm reaching up into the dark with its fluke and the other driven down into the silt; its ring standing up off the floor; and its chain rising two hundred blocks out of the dark, broken off, a length of it coiled on the floor. Beside it lies the Canticle herself: her bow half sunk, her name on her side, her decks open where she broke (cabins, a lamp still lit), her hawse pipe torn, her funnel fallen beside her. All round are the bones of what the Undertow has eaten: whales, a leviathan's skull with its jaws open, fish, divers.

    In the hollow of the anchor's crown is the Static Heart: "Warm, and beating - to no rhythm of its own. There's a picture in it, of nothing, always moving". "Keep it. Something is going to be made with it".

    After the Turning

    When the Turning plays (Chapter Nine), the Undertow comes up out of the sea by the rig, goes to the Drowned Mycelia and eats the Grove Heart, goes through the Drowned Heart in the Haemal Reach, and comes back with both in it. Its last time down it lies in the sea north-east of the rig ("on your left, looking out to sea from the helipad"), its jaws a hundred blocks off the rig: the husk, its eyes shut, spores steaming off one side and blood off the other, its heart heard from the rig, beating faster as the days go.

    For the three days it lies there nothing comes up out of the Black Below, the Grove's Heart doesn't grow back, and the Drowned Heart doesn't beat. The Watcher keeps watch at the helipad's rail. When the Harbinger has risen out of it and gone, the husk goes down into the sea.
