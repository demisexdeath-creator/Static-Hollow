    What it is

    Tune-In Town's sister venue: the service and rental floor under a dinner theatre the company shut in 1989, its revue's animatronics still down there running their show for nobody. Survive Tune-In Town's five nights and the company's pleased with you: in Studio B's Parts & Service there's a service lift (B2 - AUTHORIZED TECHNICIANS ONLY; before the five nights it won't take you). You're staff down there. The lift opens on the control booth: a desk, a row of buttons, the night crew's book on its lectern and a crescent moon in neon. From it two corridors run up into a long service hall with six wings off it (the Fortune Parlor, the Aquarium Lounge, the Roller Rink, the Banquet Kitchen, the Orchestra Pit and Parts & Service) and, at the end of the hall behind a red curtain, the Moon Room.

    The Night Bus (1.77.0) stops at the Annex too. Since 1.77.4, if its lift takes you up into a Studio B that was never built, Studio B is built round you, and its front doors take you back the way you came.

    The night shift

    Press the time clock button by the lift and keep the booth from 12 till 6 AM. Gordon Pike, the Starlight's old stage manager, talks you through it off his tapes over the PA, every line voiced and in the chat. On a server it's everyone down there's shift. There are no doors:

    • The cast come up the two corridors. The white buttons light a corridor for a moment; the yellow ones shock it, and whoever's in it goes back to their place. The shock needs a few seconds to recharge (the HUD shows which are ready). Leave one at the end of your corridor too long and it comes in.
    • Madame Marigold and Chef Gustave don't mind the shock. When one of them comes into the booth, get under the desk (crouch under it) and stay there till they've gone, and for the Chef, keep perfectly still: he listens.
    • Pearl comes up the pipe by the desk: listen for water, and press the yellow button beside it.
    • Maestro Vincent, left standing in a corridor, lifts his baton, and everyone comes faster a while. Shock him first.
    • The music box: now and then Lullabelle comes out for her evening dance, through the booth in her moon and away. When you hear her music box, hold your breath: crouch and don't move till she's gone (your breath is on the HUD).
    • Leave the booth and one of them leaves its place too, and comes after you.

    Caught, with its face in yours and its line, and you're fired: the rest of the crew carry on; all of you caught, and it's the night again. Every night done pays tickets, and each night's trophy is given the first time it's done. The cast are on the Starlight Annex's cast.

    The closing night

    The sixth night (1.75.7). The lift's cut, the house lights go, and all of them are after you. Throw the four breakers, get through the stage door at the back of the Moon Room's stage, and up the service stairs and out. Out the top, the closing credits roll: the cast, each with their act; the stage manager; the night crew (you); and good night, to the music box. Esc skips them. Earns Closing Time.

    Two-handed nights

    With two or more of you clocked in (1.75.8), the fuse box by one wing's door goes now and then, and that corridor's shocks are dead till somebody resets it. One keeps the desk, one runs; whoever's gone to a fuse has a while longer before they're missed. Take a walkie each.

    Tickets and the prize counter

    The prize counter is by the booth's west wall (1.75.7). Tickets come out of the arcade cabinets (a good score pays), from every night done, and from Tune-In tokens fed to the counter (ten tickets a token). Each player's tickets are their own. Spend them on:

    • Plushes of the cast, drawn from their own models in a softer, brighter plush texture: Madame Marigold, Pearl, Roxie, Rhonda, Maestro Vincent, Chef Gustave and Lullabelle (squeeze one and it squeaks). The Golden Lullabelle comes from the closing night.
    • The Annex's snacks and its decorations.
    • The revue's records (1.75.8; the Roller Rink's jukebox plays them by itself), and the Hound's things: his bed, treats and collars.

    The Midnight Fair's prize counter takes Annex tickets too. A complete set of plushes, trophies, Gordon's tapes or records also earns a bonus in the collector's hall (Gordon's tapes: a tenth off at the counter).

    The arcade and the claw machine

    Put a Tune-In token in a cabinet (Tune-In Town's, or one of the Annex's Star Catchers) and its game comes up on its glass, a little screen of its own, for 45 seconds:

    • Star Catcher: the basket along the bottom (A/D, the arrows or the mouse). Stars are a point, gold ones five; not the bombs.
    • Moon Hop: the moon-rabbit hops the crates (space, W, up or click), a point a crate. Trip, and it's over.
    • Pop-Up Parade: the cast's heads pop up out of the stage's trapdoors; whack them (a point, Lullabelle in gold three). Not the stagehand.

    The better it goes, the more tickets out of the slot; a jackpot at Tune-In Town's cabinets pays its prize out of the tray as well. The claw machine (1.75.8) is across the booth from the counter: steer the claw, drop it, and what it holds on to is yours.

    Gordon Pike's lost tapes

    The stage manager's own tapes, on his dictaphone, not the PA's (1.75.7): one left in each wing, a cassette on the floor with a glint over it. Each player picks up their own copy. Use one to play it, his words on screen as it plays (crouch and use it to stop). What the Starlight was, what the cast did, and why he stayed:

    1. Opening Night (the Fortune Parlor)
    2. Pearl (the Aquarium Lounge)
    3. Skate Night (the Roller Rink, behind the soda counter)
    4. The Chef (the Banquet Kitchen)
    5. The Maestro (the Orchestra Pit, on the stage)
    6. Closing Night (Parts & Service, on a workbench)

    All six earn Stage Manager's Notes. The Hound's whistle sniffs out the ones you haven't got.

    The Encore

    Find all six tapes and see the closing night through (1.75.8), and a panel in the booth's west wall slides back: Gordon's office, his desk, his shelves of tapes, and on the desk his Last Reel, the seventh tape. The house power runs through the big switch on his wall: throw it and the cast do the Starlight Revue one more time, just for you, for all of you in the Moon Room's seats. The overture; each of them in their spotlight; Lullabelle last, dancing over the stage; the bows; Gordon's goodbye. Then the power goes down for good and they rest, heads bowed at their places. The first time: her lullaby on a record, and a pile of tickets. The nights are still there to work, and the switch to throw again.

    The Starlight Supper Club

    The dinner theatre over the Annex, up the stairs from the booth by the host stand (1.75.8). Ring the bell at the pass to start a service: you're on tables tonight. Each ring, a covered plate with its ticket; carry it to that table (its number's on the sign over it) and use it there. Twelve plates, four minutes. The cast are the band on the stage, and when the band stops (a drum roll first), stand still while they take their bow: they don't like to see the staff moving. Caught moving three times and you're off the floor. Every plate pays its tip (quicker pays more); the lot served pays the night's tickets, and the first time the club's own record. All twelve earn Lovely Service.

    Gustave's Range is at the pass (1.76.0): cook static fishing's strange fish on it. See Gustave's kitchen.

    Pearl's pool

    A line cast into Pearl's pool in the Aquarium Lounge (1.75.9) reels in from static fishing's catches: strange fish, junk, odd catches and now and then something that bites. See static fishing.
